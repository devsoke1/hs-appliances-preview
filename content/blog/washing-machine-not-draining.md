You open the door at the end of a cycle and there's still water in the drum — or the machine has stopped mid-wash with an error code flashing. A washing machine that won't drain is one of the most common faults we're asked about in Feltham and across West London, and the good news is that the cause is often simple.

This guide walks through the checks you can safely do yourself, in the order we'd do them, and explains the point at which it's worth calling an engineer.

## First: make it safe

Before you touch anything:

- **Switch the machine off at the wall and unplug it.** Water and electricity don't mix.
- **Don't force the door.** Most machines keep the door locked while there's water inside. Forcing it can break the door interlock — and flood your floor.
- **Get towels and a shallow tray ready.** A full drum can hold more water than you'd expect.

## 1. Check it's not just the programme

It sounds obvious, but some programmes deliberately pause with water in the drum — for example a "rinse hold" or "anti-crease" option, or some wool and delicate cycles. Check the display and your manual. If the machine is simply waiting for you, selecting a drain or spin programme will empty it.

## 2. Look at the drain hose

Pull the machine out carefully and look at the grey drain hose at the back:

- Is it **kinked or squashed** behind the machine? This happens easily when a machine is pushed back into a tight space.
- Is it pushed **too far down the standpipe**? If the end of the hose sits below the water level in the pipe, it can siphon or back up.

## 3. Check the sink waste or standpipe

If your machine drains into the sink trap under the kitchen sink, that connection can block with grease and food debris from the sink — especially if the sink itself is draining slowly.

A classic problem after a **new sink, tap or trap has been fitted**: the plumber's blanking cap on the washing machine spigot of the new trap was never removed. If your machine stopped draining right after kitchen work, check that first.

## 4. Clean the pump filter

This is the single most common fix. Most front-loading machines have a filter behind a small flap at the bottom front corner. It catches coins, hair grips, buttons, bra wires and fluff before they reach the pump.

To clean it:

1. Make sure the machine is **off and unplugged**.
2. Lay towels down and put a shallow tray or baking dish under the flap.
3. If there's a small emergency drain tube next to the filter, pull it out and drain the water into the tray first — it's slower but much less messy.
4. **Unscrew the filter slowly, anticlockwise.** Water will come out — stop and empty the tray as needed.
5. Remove debris from the filter and the opening behind it, then rinse the filter.
6. Screw it back in firmly and straight. A cross-threaded filter will leak.

Run a drain or spin programme to test. If the machine now drains, the blockage was the problem.

## 5. Listen to the pump

Run a drain programme and listen near the bottom of the machine:

- **A normal whirring with water flowing out** — the pump is fine; any blockage is further down the line.
- **A hum but no water moving** — the pump may be jammed by something lodged in the impeller, or the pump motor may have failed.
- **Silence** — the pump isn't getting power, which can mean a failed pump, a wiring fault or a control board problem.

## 6. What the error code is telling you

Many machines show a specific code when they can't drain. Common examples include **OE** on LG machines, **5E / 5C** on Samsung, **E18 / F18** on Bosch and **F05** on Hotpoint and Indesit — but codes vary between models, so check your manual. A drain code confirms the machine knows it can't empty; it doesn't tell you whether the cause is a blockage, the pump or the electronics.

## When to call an engineer

It's time to get it looked at if:

- The filter, hose and sink connection are all clear but the machine still won't drain.
- The pump hums but doesn't move water, or makes no sound at all.
- The door won't release even after the drum has been emptied.
- The machine is tripping the electrics or you can see water leaking from underneath.

At that point the likely culprits are the drain pump, the sump hose between the drum and the pump, the pressure switch that tells the machine how much water is in the drum, or the main control board. An engineer can test each one rather than guessing — which matters, because they're very different repairs.

## How to stop it happening again

- **Check pockets** before every wash. Coins and hair grips cause more pump problems than anything else.
- **Use a wash bag** for underwired bras — loose wires are a frequent find in pump filters.
- **Don't overdose detergent.** Too much foam can trigger drain and foam errors, especially with high-efficiency machines.
- **Clean the pump filter every few months**, not just when there's a problem.
- **Run a hot maintenance wash** with a machine cleaner from time to time to clear grease and residue from the sump and hoses.
